Study 1 of 3

A first attempt at measuring

Five groups of the AI worked on 11 tasks and were scored strictly: a run only earned a point if it followed every critical rule. Skills scored higher, but there was not enough data to draw a conclusion.

Inconclusive 11 tasks, 4 repetitions each, 220 runs in total, data collection finished 14 Sep 2026

On this page

  1. Result
  2. Why this study was inconclusive
  3. What changed next
  4. Full numbers for examiners

Result

Skills scored 36.4% and the single file 27.3%, a difference of +9.1 points

Skills scored higher, but there is too little data to tell whether the difference is real or just chance (p = 0.500). So the result is inconclusive, which does not mean the two formats are equal.

Another number we looked at is the share of rules followed (RCR), where Skills was +2.3 points higher. But the plan allowed this to be tested only if the main result passed, so it was not tested.

Why this study was inconclusive

  1. Each task has 9 to 10 critical rules, and missing a single one gives zero. Most runs scored zero, and 6 of the 11 tasks ended in a tie between the two formats.
  2. A tied task tells us nothing, which left only 5 tasks that could decide the result. Even if Skills had won all 5, the result still could not be statistically significant (the smallest possible p value is 0.0625, which does not reach 0.05).
  3. The original plan was 6 repetitions, but time and quota ran short, so every group was cut to 4 repetitions using the fallback rule written in advance (Amendment 14). The cut was not made after seeing results.

What changed in the next study

All-or-nothing scoring was too harsh. The next study switched to scoring by the share of rules followed (RCRc) and added a Skills group that reads everything every time.

Groups A3 and A4

A3, text of the same length that is not rules If rules help because of their content, A3 should score close to the no rules group. If they help only because there is a lot of text, A3 should score close to the single file. In this study A3 followed every critical rule in 43.2% of runs, the single file in 27.3% and no rules in 36.4%, but this way of scoring gives zero most of the time, so the two cannot be told apart.

A4, Skills with bait comments followed every critical rule in 43.2% of runs, compared with 36.4% for regular Skills.

These two groups are not part of the main question and were not tested statistically. We dropped them in the next study to spend the time on the main question.
