# General information about the project

## Background

The research centre's system for booking time on graphics processing units began development in 2023.
Before that, bookings used a spreadsheet shared within the unit. Administrators entered data by hand
and summarised monthly usage at the end of each month by counting back through the saved files

When the number of machines grew from two to nine within two years, the old method stopped coping,
so a budget was set aside to develop a new system in the following fiscal year

The team chose to write it in TypeScript on the Node platform because the existing staff were familiar with the language,
and chose to use no external libraries at all, to reduce the long-term maintenance load on a small unit.
The second decision was reviewed again the following year and the same conclusion was reached

## Team structure

At first there were two developers working on it alongside their regular duties. Now there are four:
two looking after the core services, one looking after reports, and one coordinating with users.
One or two student assistants rotate in each semester

Progress meetings are held once a week on Wednesday mornings and take about forty minutes,
with an overall review at the end of every semester

## Release cycle

Releases are scheduled every two weeks, on alternate Fridays,
with a one working day change freeze before each release.
During semester breaks the cycle is extended to once a month because usage drops sharply

There are three environments: development, user acceptance testing, and production.
The test environment uses a copy of the data with personal information removed,
and that copy is refreshed at the start of every month

## Naming and work tracking

Work items use a prefix by category. The most common categories are bug fixes,
new features, and internal improvements that users do not see.
The last category was added later, because such work used to be mixed into the first one,
which made the quarterly bug statistics higher than they really were for a while

Users report problems through the centre's central form, which is collected and prioritised once a week.
Requests about access rights to the machines are passed directly to the system administrators,
because they are outside the scope of this software

## Past changes

In the first year the data storage model was changed once, from storing the current state of each item
to storing a sequence of events and recalculating the state when needed.
The main reason was the need to trace back who did what and when,
which was a requirement from the centre's internal audit

In the second year a usage summary report was added for laboratory heads,
and the booking list screen was adapted to work on mobile devices

The third year is gradually adding usage conditions under the centre's new policy,
which took effect at the start of the fiscal year

## Usage notes

The busiest period is the four weeks before the paper deadline of the main academic conference.
During that period the rate of fully booked slots is about three times the yearly average.
The team once considered adding a reserve queue for this period, but has not done so yet

The number of users registered in the system is currently in the low hundreds,
and about one third of them actually use it in any given month

## Location and equipment

All the machines are installed in the same room on the second floor of the research building.
The room has air conditioning separate from the rest of the building, and a backup power supply.
The system administrators inspect the machines twice a week according to the schedule posted outside the room

The two oldest machines are scheduled to be retired in the next fiscal year,
and budget for replacements has already been discussed at the annual meeting,
but there is no decision yet on which model to buy

Entering the room requires a staff card or a student card registered in advance.
The door control system is separate from this software and is managed by the facilities department

## Onboarding and handover

New team members first get access to the development environment,
then access to the other environments as their work requires.
The average settling-in time recorded in earlier rounds is about three to four weeks

The team's internal documents used to be scattered in many places, in email attachments and shared folders.
They were brought together in one place in the middle of last year and reorganised.
Some documents from before that consolidation have not yet been checked against the current state

Handover between semesters uses a one-to-one meeting of about an hour,
together with a summary note written in advance by the person handing over

## Indicators the unit tracks

The unit reports two sets of figures to management every quarter.
The first is machine utilisation compared with the total time open for booking.
The second is the number of requests outstanding for more than fifteen working days

The first set of figures was questioned at a meeting two quarters ago,
because the departments did not agree on the definition of time open for booking.
This is still waiting for a decision from the working group
